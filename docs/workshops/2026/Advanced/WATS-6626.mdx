> Build a useful understanding of a riverscape, refine it as far as necessary, and turn it into a clear, defensible plan for action. In this advanced short course, participants use Riverscapes Studio ([QRiS](https://qris.riverscapes.net)) to complete a site-scale LTPBR planning analysis for one or more potential restoration projects within a riverscape of their choosing. They assess current riverscape health, estimate relevant historical conditions, and evaluate realistic potential future conditions to prepare for LTPBR design.

### Who Should Attend

This advanced short course is designed for restoration practitioners, natural-resource professionals, graduate students, and GIS users who want to apply riverscape network models to project planning. Participants should already be comfortable working in GIS. Previous LTPBR coursework is helpful but not required.

### What You Will Learn

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Frame a planning problem** by clearly stating the project purpose, management goals, initial problems and opportunities, project area, and broader watershed context.
2. **Assemble and organize existing knowledge**—including spatial data, model outputs, imagery, observations, and stakeholder information—in a Riverscapes Studio project.
3. **Read and interpret a riverscape** by developing an early explanation of how it functions and why it is in its present condition.
4. **Refine that understanding with appropriate evidence** by mapping and comparing current, estimated historic or reference, and realistically attainable future conditions.
5. **Make transparent planning decisions** about project objectives, recovery potential, risks, constraints, opportunities, and spatial priorities while explicitly documenting assumptions and uncertainty.
6. **Evaluate the appropriateness and likely role of LTPBR** within the site's broader recovery trajectory.
7. **Prepare a defensible handoff to design** that identifies objectives, likely treatment approaches, design considerations, expected outcomes, and questions still requiring resolution.
8. **Communicate and share the planning analysis** through a concise report, presentation, published Riverscapes Studio project, and, where appropriate, a proposed project entry in PBR Explorer.

### Prerequisites

- Be comfortable with basic GIS workflows. See [Getting Oriented in QGIS](https://riverscapes.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/153000083973-getting-oriented-in-qgis) for self-paced preparation or [USU's Geospatial Collective](https://www.usu.edu/geospatial/) for formal training options.
- Be familiar with the planning concepts in [Chapter 3 of the LTPBR Design Manual](/manual/chap03). Previous completion of WATS/CEWA 5622 is helpful but not required.
- Install a [supported version of QGIS](https://qris.riverscapes.net/download) and the Riverscapes Studio (QRiS) and Riverscapes Viewer plugins before class.
- Have a Riverscapes Consortium account and arrive with a candidate project site. Existing WATS/CEWA 5622 projects may be reused and expanded.
