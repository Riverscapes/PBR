### Who Should Attend

This advanced short course is intended for restoration practitioners, natural-resource professionals, graduate students, and GIS users who want to move from a completed planning analysis to a defensible, practical LTPBR design. Participants should bring a real or realistic project site and an existing planning analysis to develop throughout the course.



### Prerequisites

- Be comfortable with basic GIS workflows. See [Getting Oriented in QGIS](https://riverscapes.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/153000083973-getting-oriented-in-qgis) for self-paced preparation or [USU's Geospatial Collective](https://www.usu.edu/geospatial/) for formal training options.
- Have completed [WATS/CEWA 6626 — Advanced LTPBR Planning](../WATS-6626) and [WATS/CEWA 5623 — Introduction to LTPBR Design](/workshops/2026/Intro/WATS-5623), or have equivalent experience. 
- Install a [supported version of QGIS](https://qris.riverscapes.net/download) and the Riverscapes Studio (QRiS) and Riverscapes Viewer plugins before class.
- Have a [Riverscapes Consortium](https://www.riverscapes.net/) account and arrive with a candidate project site and planning analysis. Existing WATS/CEWA 6626 projects may be continued into design.

## Course Overview

Design begins with the understanding, objectives, recovery potential, spatial priorities, risks, and constraints developed during planning. Here we turn those planning judgments into a practical proposal for where to act, what treatments to use, and identifying how those treatments are expected to influence riverscape processes, as well as considering logistics of how the work could be implemented.

This course helps practitioners translate a planning analysis into a defensible site-scale LTPBR design. Participants will use mapping and quantitative analysis to set or refine project objectives, select the locations and types of treatments, and describe their expected short- and medium-term effects. They will develop a desktop design while discussing how field design and field verification should refine it.

The goal is not to produce a static blueprint that presumes certainty. It is to prepare a practical design that is explicit about expected responses (design hypotheses in zones of influences), construction needs, access, staging, material sources, risks, and constraints. The resulting design should be sufficiently complete to guide implementation while remaining open to field adjustment and adaptive management.

### Relationship to the Advanced LTPBR Series

WATS/CEWA 6627 begins with the planning handoff developed in **[WATS/CEWA 6626 — Advanced LTPBR Planning](../WATS-6626)**: a defensible understanding of the riverscape, project objectives, recovery potential, spatial priorities, likely treatment approaches, risks, and constraints.

This course carries that work through detailed treatment selection and layout, expected effects, implementation considerations, and preparation of a design report. The completed design establishes explicit predictions that can subsequently be tested through riverscape monitoring and adaptive management.

---

### Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Use** Riverscapes Studio to complete LTPBR Design process for their own project.
2. **Apply** concepts of Conservation Planning Process and LTPBR Design taught in WATS/CEWA 5623, but with QRiS and quantiative mapping and analysis.
3. **Articulate and support** "complex-scale" objectives as part of design process appropriate to addressing condition impairments identified in planning phase.
4. **Design** LTPBR treatments by specify locations and type of specific LTPBR structures and actions** - and use "structure-scale" zones of influence to articulate hypothesized short and medium term responses.
5. **Formulate** a design inclusive of construction, staging, and material sourcing logistics that account for risks and constraints identified in planning phase.
6. **Develop** expectations for short and medium term responses to proposed actions in this phase of their design, and articulate how much progress towards the recovery potential identified in their planning assessment they expect this treatment to potentially achieve.
7. **Create** a full LTPBR design and report, post draft project to the Data Exchange, and if appropriate share proposed actions on PBR Explorer.

---

### Main Course Project

Each participant will complete an LTPBR Design for a project reach or riverscape of their choosing. Participants are encouraged to continue the project developed in [WATS/CEWA 6626](../WATS-6626), but may bring an equivalent planning analysis for another site.

The project will develop iteratively throughout the week:

1. **Design basis:** Review and refine the planning handoff, including project objectives, recovery potential, spatial priorities, likely treatment approaches, risks, and constraints.
2. **Treatment selection and layout:** Select treatment types and map their proposed locations, density, and configuration in relation to site conditions and design objectives.
3. **Expected effects:** Describe and, where appropriate, estimate the short- and medium-term effects of proposed treatments on riverscape conditions and processes.
4. **Implementation and refinement:** Account for access, staging, materials, construction needs, risk, constraints, and the field observations or adjustments needed before and during implementation.

In-class exercises will mix guided examples, where participants work together at a common site, with application of the same methods to each participant's own site.

### Final Products

To complete the course, participants will:

- Complete an **LTPBR Design Report**, including design objectives, proposed treatments and layout, expected effects, implementation considerations, risks, constraints, assumptions, and remaining questions.
- Create a **QRiS project** and publish it to the [Riverscapes Data Exchange](https://data.riverscapes.net/pt/riverscapesstudio).
- Attach the completed design report as a PDF [attachment](https://qris.riverscapes.net/software-help/attachments/) to the published Riverscapes Studio project and associate it with the relevant design DCE(s).
- Add proposed actions to [PBR Explorer](https://pbr.riverscapes.net) when appropriate. Projects may be kept private within a Data Exchange organization shared with the instructors.
- Give a concise presentation of the design and receive peer and instructor feedback at the end of the workshop.
- Share links to the presentation and Riverscapes Studio project in the [Advanced LTPBR Series Community](https://www.riverscapes.net/topics/45485/feed).

### Course Grading

Pass / Fail grading is based on completion of the project assignment and participation.

A passing project does not need to be a final construction package or represent a design that will actually be built. It must be a plausible design—even where assumptions are made for the purposes of the class—and demonstrate that the student has:

- Connected design objectives and treatment choices to the planning analysis and site conditions.
- Selected and mapped appropriate treatment types, locations, densities, and configurations.
- Described the expected short- and medium-term effects of the proposed actions.
- Accounted for construction needs, access, staging, material sources, risks, and site constraints.
- Distinguished evidence from assumptions and uncertainty and identified needs for field verification or adjustment.
- Completed and shared the required design report, Riverscapes Studio project, presentation, and associated links.

Simple: do the work - pass.
