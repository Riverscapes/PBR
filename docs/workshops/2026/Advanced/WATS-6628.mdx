### Who Should Attend

This advanced short course is intended for restoration practitioners, natural-resource professionals, graduate students, researchers and GIS users who want to design, organize, analyze, and communicate riverscape monitoring. Participants may focus on a restoration project or on broader questions about riverscape condition and change.

- Be comfortable with basic GIS workflows. See [Getting Oriented in QGIS](https://riverscapes.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/153000083973-getting-oriented-in-qgis) for self-paced preparation or [USU's Geospatial Collective](https://www.usu.edu/geospatial/) for formal training options.
- Previous LTPBR coursework is helpful but not required.
- Install a [supported version of QGIS](https://qris.riverscapes.net/download) and the Riverscapes Studio (QRiS) and Riverscapes Viewer plugins before class.
- Have a [Riverscapes Consortium](https://www.riverscapes.net/) account and arrive with a candidate monitoring site, project, or question. Existing planning or design projects may be continued into monitoring.

## Course Overview

Monitoring begins with a question — not a method. Useful monitoring makes clear what is being observed, why it matters, how observations made at different places and times can be compared, and what evidence would support, challenge, or refine an interpretation of riverscape condition or project performance. In a restoration context, the question is often:
> Did the restoration actions achieve the desired outcomes?

This is *action-effectiveness* monitoring, and is done best in the context of the restoration plan and design. Monitoring can be based on a before - after design, or a B-A-C-I before, after, control intervention alnalysis. 

For basic *status and trend* monitoring, repeated observations over time are used to detect changes in riverscape condition, independent of specific restoration or management actions. A question in this case might be:
> Is the riverscape condition improving, declining, or remaining stable over time?

This course helps practitioners turn monitoring questions into organized, repeatable observations and defensible analyses. Participants will use QRiS protocols to guide the collection and organization of monitoring data. They will consider how method selection and sampling design depend on the purpose of the monitoring.

The goal is not simply to collect more data. It is to use fit-for-purpose evidence to learn whether conditions are changing, whether restoration actions are working as expected, and how planning, design, implementation, or future monitoring should adjust in response.

### Relationship to the Advanced LTPBR Series

WATS/CEWA 6628 provides the **learn and adjust** portion of the advanced series. It can test the understanding and expectations developed during planning and the design hypotheses made explicit during design, including anticipated responses within treatment zones of influence.

Monitoring also informs future projects. By organizing comparable observations and documenting what was learned, participants create evidence that can refine subsequent planning, design, screening, prioritization, and adaptive management—not only at the monitored site, but across a broader portfolio of work.

---

### Learning Outcomes

By the end of this course, students will be able to:

1. **Use** Riverscapes Studio to **populate** Data Capture Events and **produce** raw monitoring observations at snapshots in time.
2. **Develop** a field monitoring form that aligns with a protocol in QRiS and **learn** how to import data from field forms as well as existing datasets into QRiS.
3. **Apply** different protocols within Data Capture Events.
4. **Analyze** monitoring data through time and space, by building riverscape appropriate sample frames and performing analyses to calculate metrics of data produced in data capture events.
5. **Develop** an **understanding** and familiarity with differences between evaluation of riverscape inventory, assesssment and monitoring for status and trend monitoring, versus baseline monitoring, versus action-effectiveness monitoring of riverscape restoration or management actions.
6. **Create** a monitoring report, and supporting QRiS Project and post to the Data Exchange.

---

### Main Course Project

Each participant will complete a Riverscape Monitoring Analysis for a site or restoration project of their choosing. Participants may continue a project developed in another advanced LTPBR course or bring an existing monitoring question and dataset or invent a new one.

The project will develop iteratively throughout the week:

1. **Question and purpose:** Define what needs to be learned, why it matters, what decisions it could inform, and which type of monitoring is appropriate.
2. **Monitoring design:** Identify indicators, spatial and temporal sampling, methods, field forms, existing information, and the evidence needed to answer the question.
3. **Organization and analysis:** Bring observations into QRiS, document their context, and calculate or summarize appropriate measurements and metrics across locations or time periods.
4. **Interpretation and learning:** Evaluate what the evidence supports, what remains uncertain, how the project or understanding should adjust, and what future monitoring is warranted.

In-class exercises will mix guided examples, where participants work together with common data, with application of the same methods to each participant's own site or question.

### Final Products

To complete the course, participants will:

- Complete a **Riverscape Monitoring Report** that documents the monitoring purpose and questions, methods, observations, analyses, results, assumptions, uncertainty, and implications for future action or learning.
- Create a **QRiS project** and publish it to the [Riverscapes Data Exchange](https://data.riverscapes.net/pt/riverscapesstudio).
- Attach the completed monitoring report as a PDF [attachment](https://qris.riverscapes.net/software-help/attachments/) to the published Riverscapes Studio project and associate it with the relevant monitoring DCE(s).
- Give a concise presentation of the monitoring analysis and receive peer and instructor feedback at the end of the workshop.
- Share links to the presentation and Riverscapes Studio project in the [Advanced LTPBR Series Community](https://www.riverscapes.net/topics/45485/feed).

### Course Grading

Pass / Fail grading is based on completion of the project assignment and participation.

A passing project does not need to resolve every uncertainty or rely on a complete, long-term dataset. It must represent a plausible monitoring application—even where example observations or assumptions are used for the purposes of the class—and demonstrate that the student has:

- Framed a clear monitoring question and connected it to a monitoring purpose and management need.
- Selected appropriate indicators, methods, spatial units, and time periods.
- Organized and documented monitoring observations in QRiS.
- Used appropriate measurements or summary metrics to analyze patterns or change.
- Distinguished evidence from interpretation, assumptions, and uncertainty.
- Explained what was learned and how it could inform future action, adjustment, or monitoring.
- Completed and shared the required monitoring report, Riverscapes Studio project, presentation, and associated links.

Simple: do the work - pass.
